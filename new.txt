Your system would investigate:
Why did the Indian market become bullish or bearish? What conditions existed before the move? Have similar conditions occurred during the last 4–5 years? What happened afterward?

This could become a separate research engine that feeds intelligence into your existing algo-trading system.

My recommendation: build this as a separate research module, not directly inside the strategy execution engine.

0. The one goal, and how we know it worked (added 2026-11-20)
Every phase must move this one number:

  "Does skipping entries on days the engine marks as poor improve the
   paper/replay results of the existing strategies, after costs?"

- Success: the filtered strategy beats the unfiltered one on data the
  engine never saw (out-of-sample), after brokerage, taxes and slippage,
  with at least 30 skipped trades.
- Every external data category below is REQUIRED scope (operator decision
  2026-11-20): it is collected, stored point-in-time and shown in the
  weekly report, whether or not it helps the strategies.
- What is NOT automatic: a category changes how strategies trade only

  report as context, and is not used as a filter.

3. The most important research: discover historical market conditions
This is where I'd spend the most effort.

Don't only ask what happened on a bullish day. Find the conditions that existed before the bullish move.

For example, imagine your research engine discovers a historical pattern:
Illustrative research finding, not an actual backtest result
Bullish historical setup
- Nasdaq previous session: positive
- GIFT NIFTY: positive gap indication
- FII index futures: net long positioning increasing
- India VIX: declining
- Bank NIFTY: outperforming NIFTY
- NIFTY: above VWAP
- Market breadth: positive

Research questions
- How many times did this combination occur?
- What percentage resulted in positive 30-minute returns?
- What was the median return?
- What was the maximum adverse movement?
- Did the pattern work in both trending and volatile markets?
- Did it survive transaction costs?

Do the same for bearish and sideways regimes.

One very important distinction: correlation is not causation. The engine should report that certain conditions preceded a move, not claim that one news headline caused it without supporting evidence.

Don't attempt all ten categories at once.

cheapest test of the goal in section 0 comes first. Expensive data (news,
macro, ML) comes only after price data alone has been tried. Each phase is
added to docs/PLAN.md as tasks before it starts.

     day's option contracts, India VIX).
   - Save the option chain (OI, IV, PCR) every 5 minutes for NIFTY and
     BANKNIFTY. Expired contracts cannot be fetched later.
   - Fetch the remaining 4 years of daily bars for the 224 F&O symbols.
   Pass: a week of complete archives; 5 years of daily bars stored.

   - Weekly Market Conditions Report, every week.
   Pass: four weekly reports delivered on time, every number traceable
   to its source and timestamp; each tested pattern has a recorded
   verdict.

   - Only on features that passed Phase 4.
   - Walk-forward testing, probability calibration.
6. Phase 6: Connect to the algo
   - Patterns that passed become a filter the Risk Engine reads

   - Paper trading for at least 20 sessions before anything real.
7. Three important things I would not compromise on
A. Avoid look-ahead bias.

Suppose FII data is published after market close. Your 10:00 AM strategy must not use that day's FII figure.

Every feature must have an actual availability timestamp.

B. Avoid overfitting.

If you discover that a particular candle pattern worked 80% of the time in 2022, that doesn't mean it will work in 2026.

Use:
- Training: earlier years.
- Validation: subsequent periods.
- Final testing: completely unseen periods.
- Walk-forward testing across different market regimes.
Include brokerage, taxes, slippage and execution delays.

Testing many patterns guarantees some will look good by luck. If 100
patterns are tested, about 5 will pass a 95% test even when none is real.
So:
- Register each hypothesis before testing it (the existing hypothesis
  registry).
- Correct for the number of tests (Holm correction, already in the
  validation gate).
- Minimum 30 occurrences before any result is shown as evidence.
- Report the number of patterns tried next to every pattern that passed.

C. Don't confuse explanation with prediction.

Your AI might explain that a market rally coincided with positive global sentiment and institutional buying.

That does not establish that the same combination will predict tomorrow's rally.

Keep two separate outputs:
1. Historical explanation: Why did the market move?
2. Predictive research: What conditions have historically preceded similar moves?
This distinction is essential.

Outcomes to calculate

How it is tested (so it is a measurement, not a story):
- Features are numbers, each scaled the same way: distance from VWAP in
  ATR, EMA gap, VIX change, gap %, BANKNIFTY vs NIFTY strength,
  minutes since open.
- Similar = the nearest past moments at the same time of day, at least
  one session apart, from before the moment being judged.
- Fewer than 30 similar moments: say "not enough history", not a verdict.
- Benchmark: compare against random past moments at the same time of day.
  If "similar" does not predict better than random, the engine says so.

10. Weekly Market Conditions Report (Phase 4)
When: every Saturday 10:00 IST, covering the week just closed and the
week ahead. Delivered to the dashboard (Research tab) and Telegram.

Sections:
1. One-paragraph summary: what moved the Indian market this week, and the
   engine's reading for next week (bullish / bearish / sideways /
   uncertain), with the evidence count behind it.
2. Global markets: US, Asia, GIFT NIFTY, US VIX, week's change.
3. Currency and rates: USD/INR, Dollar Index, US/India 10Y, central banks.
4. Commodities: crude, gold, metals, energy, with a note on the sectors
   each one historically moved (only patterns that passed testing).
5. FII/DII: weekly cash flows, participant-wise OI change, long/short ratio.
6. Economic data: releases this week vs expectations; releases next week.
7. News and geopolitical events: the week's top events with publish time
   and the measured market reaction.
8. Calendar next week: holidays, expiries, RBI/Fed, budget, results,
   festivals, with how such days historically changed volatility.
9. India VIX and derivatives: VIX level/change, OI build-up by strike,
   PCR, IV and skew changes, rollover.
10. Other factors: breadth, sector rotation, earnings, block deals, IPOs,
    government spending, monsoon/weather, shipping, semiconductor cycle,
    regulatory decisions, credit conditions, positioning.
11. Pattern scoreboard: which researched conditions are active now, each
    with occurrences, hit rate, average move, and its verdict (passed /
    failed / not enough history).
12. Strategy impact: last week's paper results per strategy, and what the
    filter would have changed.

Rules for the report:
- Every number shows its source and the time it became public.
- Explanation ("why it moved") is kept apart from prediction ("what
  preceded similar moves"), as section 7C says.
- A pattern without at least 30 occurrences is shown as context, never
  as a forecast.
- Missing data is shown as missing, never filled in.

I particularly like this direction because you're moving from building a bot that simply reacts to indicators toward building a system that studies market behavior.

The real objective shouldn't be to predict every market movement. It should be to discover which market conditions give your strategies a measurable statistical advantage, and when they should stay out.
